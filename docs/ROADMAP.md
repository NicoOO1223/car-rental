# Roadmap

Status as of 2026-09-27. Update this file whenever a layer ships or a decision changes. Until now the layer scheme lived only in planning sessions and commit messages, so treat this as the single record.

## Direction

The product is being built to sell to other rental operators.

- **Customer:** independent rental operators (B2B).
- **Hosting:** one separate stack per customer first. True multi-tenancy is deferred until real customers exist.
- **Email and SMS:** deferred by decision.
- **Storefront:** a public booking site for renters is in scope.

The earlier layers assumed an internal, staff-only, single-business tool. That assumption no longer holds for the storefront or for per-customer business identity.

## Next layers

Each layer gets its own planning pass that re-explores the code before any build starts.

**L12 Production hardening.** Shipped and live on `demo`.
- Perimeter: the database no longer accepts the internet. The backup job opens a firewall rule for its own runner and removes it. Containers run as non-root with health checks. Docs are off in production, security headers are on for both API and frontend (the frontend CSP is Report-Only for now), and CORS is limited to what the app uses.
- Secrets and access: the signing key is generated by the bootstrap script. The app can use a database role that cannot change the schema, with migrations run by a separate login.
- Auth: five wrong passwords lock an account for 15 minutes, with a per-IP budget on top. Password reset, deactivation and role changes revoke existing sessions. Deactivated accounts cannot log in.
- Payments: a signed Stripe webhook with event dedupe, one shared rule set for payment state (a refunded or succeeded payment never moves backwards, which also fixes confirm-payment resurrecting a refunded booking), idempotency keys on creates, refunds and captures.
- Performance: indexes on the hot queries, connection pool settings, availability in one query, cheaper dashboard, server-side paging and search for reservations and customers, and one request for the fleet timeline.
- Quality: the test suite runs on its own `_test` database and refuses any other. CI uses the production Postgres major, deploys are smoke-tested, Dependabot and a weekly dependency audit are on, and the repo has a root README.
- Monitoring: health and readiness probes, plus alert rules for restarts, server errors, database CPU, storage and failed connections.

Not done, on purpose:
- **Private database network.** The database still admits Azure services broadly (the Container Apps outbound IPs are too many to allow-list). A VNet with a private endpoint is the full fix and needs its own change.
- **Staging environment.** Moved to L14: the operator's own demo stack becomes the canary that deploys first.
- **Pagination for the remaining lists** (cars, maintenance, users). They are small and bounded by the size of the business.

**L13 Business profile and localization.** Shipped and live on `demo`.
- Business profile in Configuration: name, legal name, address, phone, email, VAT ID, timezone, formatting locale and agreement terms, plus an uploaded logo stored in the database. A public branding endpoint lets the sign-in page show the name and logo. The sidebar, sign-in page, tab title and agreement all use them; nothing hardcodes "Car Rental" any more.
- Locations are administered in the app: add, edit and retire. Each has its own timezone. A location still holding vehicles or open reservations cannot be retired, and new bookings cannot use a retired one.
- Days follow timezones instead of UTC: the dashboard's today counts, revenue by day, fleet status for a date, reservation day filters, maintenance and inspection dates. The booking wizard and edit modal turn "10:00" into an instant using the pickup or return location's timezone, which fixes bookings made from a different timezone than the airport. Times, dates and money follow the business locale.
- Identity is country neutral: a national ID number is optional (an ID document number also identifies the customer), the PESEL checksum only runs for a Polish customer who enters one, and the country list is every ISO country in the business language.
- VAT and invoices: one VAT rate, with prices either including VAT or having it added. A rental payment issues a numbered invoice in the same transaction, from whichever path records it. Numbers come from a locked counter, so the sequence has no gaps. An invoice copies the seller, customer, lines and amounts, and never changes. A refund issues a credit note.

Decisions and gaps to know about:
- **Return charges are not invoiced.** Late fees, fuel and damage charges take payment but produce no invoice, because how VAT applies to them (damage compensation in particular) is a question for an accountant. Payments taken before this shipped also have no invoice, and refunding one issues no credit note.
- Only formats follow the locale in L13. The words are translated by L13b below.
- The fleet timeline draws days by the viewer's own calendar and the business "today". A day with a clock change can be an hour off in that drawing only.
- One VAT rate for the whole business, not per item.
- Invoice numbers are one series with a configurable prefix, and credit notes share the sequence under the prefix CN. Some countries require a separate or yearly series; confirm before the first customer.
- Historic records keep their times: naive timestamps written before this were UTC, and are still read as UTC.

Deployment notes (done for `demo`):
1. ~~Push.~~ The migrations changed no behaviour on their own: timezone stays UTC, VAT stays 0, the brand stays "Car Rental" until set.
2. As an admin, open Configuration, Business and set the name, details, timezone and format. Set each location's timezone under Locations.
3. Set the VAT rate and price setting once known. The first invoice number starts the sequence.

**L13b Interface and document language.** Shipped and live on `demo`. English, Italian and Polish.
- Each person picks their own interface language in the sidebar (labelled "Language", not "Settings"), saved on their account. This is the first item of the future personal-preferences layer. A business default, set under Configuration, Business, applies to anyone who has not chosen, and to the sign-in page for a first visit. A signed-in person never inherits the language another person used on the same device.
- Every screen reads its text from `frontend/messages/{en,it,pl}.json`: whole sentences with plural forms (Polish has four), so nothing is glued together from fragments. Statuses, roles, categories and audit actions show translated names while the stored values are unchanged. A missing translation shows English, never a bare key.
- Server errors carry a stable code and their values (`{detail, code, params}`), and the interface words them in the reader's language. The English `detail` stays for other clients, and a test fails if a service raises a sentence without a code.
- Printed documents use a separate business document language. The agreement follows the current setting. An invoice is stored with the language it was issued in, so a reprint never changes, and a credit note uses its invoice's language. Documents use the bundled DejaVu Sans, since the built-in font cannot print Polish letters. Dates keep the year-first layout with the zone name in every language, on purpose, so they cannot be misread.
- CI runs `npm run check:messages --strict`: the three catalogs have the same keys and placeholders, every key used in code exists and is given the values it needs, every server error code has a message, and no text is left hardcoded in a screen. A development-only pseudo-language (`Pseudo (dev)` in the picker) marks every extracted string, so unextracted text is easy to spot.

Decisions and gaps to know about:
- **Italian and Polish were drafted by Claude and have not been reviewed by a native speaker.** This matters most for the agreement and invoice wording, which can carry legal weight. Some countries require specific phrases on invoices; confirm before the first real invoice.
- **Dates on screen follow the business format, not the interface language.** Set the business format under Configuration, Business (for example Italiano) so weekdays and months are worded to match.
- Extras' names and descriptions are business data, so each extra has optional Italian and Polish wording in its edit form, and English is the fallback for any field left empty. The starting extras come with Italian and Polish wording from the migration. Screens, bookings, the agreement and invoices use the wording for the language showing (the document language for printed documents).
- A custom agreement text is printed exactly as the admin wrote it, in whatever language that is.
- Not covered: languages beyond these three (adding one is a catalog file, see `docs/TRANSLATING.md`), and right-to-left layout.

Deployment notes:
1. ~~Push.~~ The migrations default everything to English, so nothing changed on deploy.
2. As an admin, set the default interface language and the document language under Configuration, Business.
3. **Still open:** have a native Italian and Polish speaker review the catalogs (`docs/TRANSLATING.md`) before customers rely on them.

**L13c Fleet cost, insurance and notifications.** Shipped and live on `demo`.
- A maintenance task can carry a cost, typed in when it is logged and replaceable when a technical inspection is completed. Empty means not entered, not zero. The maintenance list and each vehicle's history show it. This is the input for the cost tracking to come.
- A vehicle has an insured-since date, an insured-until date and an insurance cost for that period, edited with the rest of its condition.
- Notifications are worked out from the data each time, never stored: a vehicle away from its home airport, an inspection due soon or overdue, insurance ending soon or ended. A bell in the sidebar shows the count (red when something is urgent) and opens a list that links to each vehicle. Items disappear when the thing is fixed, so nothing needs marking as read. The lead time is Configuration, System, "Reminder window (days)", 30 by default.
- Staff notifications are in-app only. Renters get a confirmation, a cancellation notice and a receipt by email once a stack's sending domain is verified (see deploy/PROVISIONING.md, Email). Later layers added the rest of the email set (pickup reminder, agreement copy, return summary, refund notice, and three emails to the business); see deploy/PROVISIONING.md, Email. SMS and overdue-return reminders are not built.
- Not covered: a cost on cleaning or non-inspection completions beyond what was entered when logging, and cost reports.

**L14 Per-customer stack automation.** Built, pushed and rolled out to the live `demo` stack (the canary).
- One image serves every stack. The frontend reads its API address, Stripe publishable key and error-tracking address from the container's environment when it runs, hands them to the browser from the layout, and builds its Content-Security-Policy per request. The Docker image takes no build arguments.
- Stacks are described by `deploy/stacks/<slug>.json` (no secrets), checked by tests: exactly one canary, no shared resource groups, business settings validated with the admin screen's own rules. The live stack is adopted as `demo`, the canary.
- The container registry moved to its own resource group (`deploy/shared.bicep`), so deleting a stack never deletes it.
- `deploy/provision.ps1 -Stack <slug>` creates or updates a whole stack: infrastructure, secrets (kept out of the repo), images, both apps, the Stripe webhook (created with the customer's own key), a GitHub Environment with its own CI login and only the Azure roles it needs, the first administrator and business profile (applied by the backend on first start, once), the least-privilege database role, and a smoke test. It supports `-DryRun`. `deprovision.ps1` removes a stack and refuses the canary.
- The release builds once, deploys to the canary, smoke-tests it, then deploys to the other stacks one at a time. Backups and restore drills run per stack. The smoke test also checks that the frontend is configured for its own stack's API.
- Details are in `deploy/PROVISIONING.md`.

Rollout done on the demo stack: the shared registry exists and both apps run from it, the `stack-demo` GitHub Environment and CI login exist, the first canary-first release deployed and passed its smoke test, and backups and the restore drill run through the new pipeline. The old registry in the demo resource group is still there and can be deleted.

Closed at the same time (the L12 checklist had never been applied to the live stack):
- The internet-wide database firewall rule is deleted. The backup job opens and removes its own rule, using a role that could not be created before because it listed an action Azure does not have.
- The backend has health probes, the migration login, and the least-privilege database role.
- The Stripe webhook endpoint is registered for the live stack, and the route now verifies signatures.
- The restore drill had been failing daily since L12. It now restores the newest backup, runs the migrations, checks the data, and boots the backend on it.

Proven with a second stack (`acme`, provisioned, verified and deprovisioned again — nothing from it is left running): a different currency (GBP), timezone (Europe/London) and locale (en-GB) applied automatically on first start from its manifest alone; the normal push-triggered release pipeline deployed to it correctly alongside `demo`; it restored from its own backup in the drill; its database sits on the same least-privilege role as `demo`.

This surfaced a real subscription limit worth knowing about: **an Azure subscription can be capped at one Container Apps environment**, in total or per region (a free-trial restriction). `sharedEnvironmentStack` in a manifest is the stopgap — a stack joins another one's environment instead of creating its own, keeping its database, backups and everything else entirely its own, with its app names prefixed by its own slug to avoid colliding with the other stack's. **Not the normal case**: every real customer stack still gets its own environment; reach for this only if the same quota wall comes up again, and ask Azure to raise it (or move to a paid tier) rather than relying on this long-term. Details in `deploy/PROVISIONING.md`.

Not done, on purpose:
- **Monitoring alerts are configured but not yet deployed.** The alert email is in `deploy/stacks/demo.json`; run `provision.ps1 -Stack demo` to deploy the rules.
- Custom domains are not automated.

**L15 Public storefront.** Shipped and live on `demo`, switched off. A separate Next.js app (`storefront/`), a `shared/` folder of dependency-free code both frontends import, and the backend's `/api/public/*`. First provisioned by hand (`provision.ps1`) so the live checks could run for real ahead of a push; the release pipeline has since deployed it too (backend, staff frontend, storefront and the sweeper job all rolled out together), and a real gap the first attempt caught — the CI identity had no role covering Container Apps *jobs*, a different resource type from a Container App — is fixed.
- **Booking, without an account.** A renter searches by place and dates, picks an offer (cars that look the same to a renter — make, model, spec, price — are grouped so no plate or internal id is ever exposed), adds extras, gives their details, accepts the terms and privacy notice, and pays a share online with Stripe. The rest is paid at pickup. A brand-new `Customer` row is created every time, with the terms/privacy version and timestamp recorded.
- **Confirmation is automatic.** One idempotent success path (the Stripe webhook, the browser's own post-payment check, and a sweeper job, whichever notices first) confirms the booking and issues an advance invoice for the amount collected. A payment that arrives after the hold ran out is honoured if the car is still free, refunded in full if it was not.
- **Holds and races.** An unpaid booking holds its car for an admin-set number of minutes as `AWAITING_PAYMENT`, then expires; a new `car-rental-sweeper` Container Apps job (every 5 minutes) frees expired holds and cancels their Stripe payments. Two requests for the same car are serialised with a row lock, closing a real double-booking gap the audit found. Availability now follows where a car will actually be (a one-way return moves it) and a car's live status no longer gates a future booking — only overlapping reservations and maintenance do.
- **After booking:** an on-screen reference (`ABCD-2345`) and, for later, a lookup page (reference plus email) that hands the browser a 60-minute token scoped only to that one booking. A renter can cancel their own booking from there.
- **Cancellation policy.** The online share is refunded in full up to an admin-set cutoff before pickup, and kept after it (and always kept for a no-show). Staff can override the policy for one cancellation, and can mark a no-show.
- **Bot and abuse protection.** Cloudflare Turnstile on booking and lookup, per-address rate limits on every public endpoint, and database-backed caps (3 live holds per address, 2 per email, a global ceiling) that survive a restart. A stack with no Turnstile secret refuses to take bookings in production rather than run open.
- **Staff side.** The reservation list and detail page show the online booking, its reference, what was paid, and the cancellation deadline; check-in shows what was already paid online and takes only the remainder (or settles a fully-prepaid rental with no payment at all); a new Configuration → Online booking page holds the on/off switch, the share percentage, the cancellation window, the hold length and the terms/privacy text; a per-car "offered online" switch; a notification for new online bookings.
- **Languages:** en, it, pl, same as the staff app, each behind its own `/en`, `/it`, `/pl` route.
- **Verification:** the whole backend suite, a repo Playwright spec (`frontend/e2e/storefront.spec.ts`) that books, pays with a Stripe test card, has staff take the remainder and complete the rental, and separately cancels a booking and checks the refund — skipped automatically where no test-mode Stripe key is configured.

Decisions and gaps to know about:
- **The demo stack's backend runs with zero minimum replicas** to save cost, so it occasionally takes 10-30+ seconds to wake up. The storefront's own public-facing fetches were made to tolerate that (a 25s timeout, not the 5s that first showed visitors a "having trouble" page instead of the correct "booking is off" one). **To do once a real customer exists:** give that stack's backend a minimum of one replica (`-BackendMinReplicas 1` in `provision.ps1`, already the default for a new customer manifest) so a visitor never meets a cold start at all.
- **The storefront's Content-Security-Policy is Report-Only**, the same posture the staff app started in (see the L12 checklist). Watch its reports for a while on a real stack before switching it to enforcing.
- **Italian and Polish storefront text is Claude's draft**, not yet reviewed by a native speaker — the same gap as L13b, now covering a second catalog (`storefront/messages/*.json`).
- **Owner decisions still open, matching the plan:** the advance-invoice document name and wording per country (Italy "fattura di acconto", Poland "faktura zaliczkowa" — confirm with an accountant), the VAT treatment and wording of an online share kept after a late cancellation, whether a zero-remainder final invoice is issued, and the "or similar" wording next to an offered car.
- Not covered: a custom domain for the storefront (same gap as L14), and the real legal text itself (L16).

1. ~~Push.~~ The migration is additive and defaults `storefront_enabled` to false, so nothing changed for an existing stack until an admin turns it on.
2. ~~Provision the storefront app and the sweeper job~~ (`deploy/provision.ps1 -Stack <slug> -TurnstileSiteKey ... -TurnstileSecretKey ...`, needs a Turnstile widget created for that stack's storefront address first; see `deploy/PROVISIONING.md`). Done for `demo`.
3. **Still open for `demo`:** as an admin, add the terms and privacy text (or links) and set the share percentage, cancellation window and hold length under Configuration, Online booking, then switch it on. It is provisioned but still switched off.

**L11 Visual redesign.** Shipped and live on `demo`, through the normal push-triggered pipeline. It added two migrations (the brand colour and the time to confirm a request), both with defaults that change nothing until set.
- **Brand colour.** Each business sets one accent colour in Configuration, Business (near-black by default, so nothing changes until they pick one). It colours the main actions and highlights in the staff app and on the storefront. `lib/accent.ts` (one copy per app) works out the text colour that goes on it and a version dark enough to use as text, so a pale choice stays readable.
- **Design tokens.** Every colour in the staff app goes through a token in `app/globals.css`; `npm run check:colors` (in CI) fails on a raw palette class. Besides the greys and the accent there are two fixed tints that never follow the brand colour: warning (waiting on staff) and danger (something wrong). The staff app uses Plus Jakarta Sans, as the storefront does.
- **Tables.** One row design, each row its own card with a hover highlight. A View menu per table sets row height and optional columns. Every list sorts from its column headers; the lists the server pages (reservations, customers, maintenance) sort on the server, with the order kept in the address.
- **Reservations.** Five columns instead of seven. Booked time is shown as an age and sorts both ways. Configuration, System has "time to confirm a request" (24 hours by default, 0 for off); a pending request close to or past it is flagged, and a tile counts the requests awaiting confirmation.
- **Fleet.** The fleet page opens on an "All vehicles" view (cards or a table, filtered by location and status); the schedule is one click away.
- **Shell and pages.** New sidebar, phone top bar with a notifications bell, sign-in page, and one heading scale and card style across the pages. On phones, filters collapse behind a button.

Not done, on purpose:
- **Dark mode.** The app stays light-only.
- **A layout rethink of the detail pages** (reservation, car, customer, check-in, check-out). They were restyled with the shared tokens, cards and headings, but their structure is unchanged; some information still repeats between sections and on a phone the approve and reject buttons sit at the bottom of a long page. Dropped on purpose, not forgotten.
- **A sticky table header.** It conflicts with the sideways scrolling that keeps a wide table from being cut off.
- **Italian and Polish wording** added with this layer is a draft, like the rest of those catalogs.
- **Small gaps.** The email log is not sortable, and the maintenance list has no "Sort by" list for phones.

**Vehicle classes can be turned off.** Configuration, Classes has a switch, on by default. Off, every car is shown and booked on its own; the classes and the cars' links to them are kept and come back when it is turned on.
- **Booking site.** Cars are offered individually (cars that look the same still group, as classless cars always have), with no class words or "or similar". A booking holds the car the renter chose, and staff cannot swap it as they can a class booking. The car's own "offered online" switch decides what is shown; a class's switch no longer has a say.
- **Staff.** A booking must name its car (booking a class is refused with `reservation.classes_off`, and the new-reservation screen drops the "assign later" choice). Class names leave the fleet screens. A car can then be priced on its own under Vehicle rates, which takes it out of its class, since a class is what shares one price.
- **Existing bookings.** A class booking made earlier keeps its provisional car and the swap rules.

**Request, approve, pay.** A booking, from staff or from the booking site, starts as a request; staff approve it; if an advance is due the renter is asked for it and the car is held until a deadline; paying it confirms the booking.
- **Statuses.** No new status: PENDING (a request) → AWAITING_PAYMENT (approved, advance asked; amber, with the deadline) → CONFIRMED (paid, waived or recorded by hand; a green "Advance paid" tag). The hold, sweeper and advance invoice are the ones the website's instant booking already used.
- **Settings** (Configuration, Online booking): how bookings are confirmed (`INSTANT`, today's behaviour and the default for existing stacks, or `APPROVAL`, the default for a new stack), the online share (now 0 to 100: 0 means nothing before pickup) and the hours an approved booking waits for its advance.
- **Paying.** The email carries a link to the booking page with a payment-only token in the fragment (it can see the booking and pay, not cancel). The card form needs the booking site, Stripe and online booking switched on; without them approving still holds the car and staff record the advance by hand (cash or transfer).
- **Staff.** Approve with the advance or without; record an advance received; send the request again; give 24 more hours (also reopens a lapsed booking if its car is still free); copy the payment link; a tile and filter for bookings awaiting payment.
- **Emails.** Three new: request received, payment requested, payment window ended (16 in all). A declined request is still silent.
- **Every booking has a reference** now, so a staff booking can be looked up and linked to like any other.

**Price adjustments.** A reservation can carry a signed adjustment line with a reason, on top of rate, fees and extras; VAT and the total follow it and it prints on the payment screen, invoice and agreement. Staff may add one; managers and admins may also set the final total or keep the agreed price when the car changes. Only before any payment is taken; audited.

## Marketing site

A separate app, `marketing/`, sells the product: a request-a-demo page (form, bot checks, email through Azure Communication Services), public plans by fleet size, real screenshots of a fictional demo business, English only for now. It is **built and deployable but not live**: the brand, prices, contact details and legal pages are placeholders until the owner decides, and the site is never indexable while they are (`npm run check:launch` lists what is missing). Design brief approved 2026-10-05; see `marketing/README.md` and the "marketing site" section of `deploy/PROVISIONING.md`.

It claims only shipped features. Known gaps it states plainly: no SMS, no scooter category, no custom domain for a stack's storefront, no Italian SDI or Polish KSeF e-invoicing, Italian and Polish text not yet natively reviewed.

## Naming rule

Admin-editable business rules are called **Configuration**. The name **Settings** is reserved for a future user-facing layer covering personal preferences, notification preferences and self-service data deletion, which needs email delivery first.
